Does iPhone have clipboard history?

What happens to copied text, what you can recover, and three ways to keep the text you use again.

By Javokhir, creator of Typez · Published November 12, 2026
This guide is published by Typez, an iPhone keyboard app currently in App Store review.

iPhone does not have a built-in clipboard history you can open to browse earlier copies. The standard clipboard holds your current copied item; copying something else replaces it. To keep text for later, save it in a note, create a Text Replacement shortcut, or deliberately import it into a saved-text keyboard such as Typez. A keyboard cannot recover an older copy it never saved.

Where is the clipboard on iPhone?

The clipboard is a temporary place for what you copy or cut. It is not a folder in Files, an album, or a screen in Settings. In Apple's standard keyboard, there is no clipboard-history button.

To see your current copied text, open a note or another editable text field, touch and hold in the field, and choose Paste. Only do this in a place where you are comfortable saving that text. If you copied a picture or another kind of item, whether you can paste it depends on the destination app.

Apple's Universal Clipboard lets you copy on one Apple device and paste on another when its requirements are met. It does not provide a browsable history of everything you copied.

Can you recover text after copying something else?

Usually, you need to return to the original source and copy the text again. iPhone has no built-in list of earlier clipboard items to restore from.

If you copied an address, then copied a link, the normal Paste action gives you the link. Installing a clipboard app afterward will not bring back that address. Check the original message, web page, document, or any app where you already pasted or saved it.

Undo may help if you accidentally changed text inside a document, but it is not a system clipboard-history feature.

Three ways to save copied text on iPhone

1. Paste it into Notes

For an occasional address, quote, or link, a note is often enough:

  1. Select the text in its original app and tap Copy.
  2. Open Notes and create or open a note.
  3. Touch and hold in the note, then tap Paste.
  4. When you need it again, copy the saved text from the note.

This is manual storage, not automatic clipboard history. The advantage is that you decide exactly what to keep. Your Notes account and sync settings determine where the note is stored.

2. Use Apple's Text Replacement for short, repeated phrases

If you repeatedly type the same phrase, you may not need a clipboard tool. Text Replacement lets a short shortcut expand into longer text.

  1. Open Settings → General → Keyboard → Text Replacement.
  2. Tap the + button.
  3. Enter the full text in Phrase and a memorable abbreviation in Shortcut.
  4. Tap Save, then try the shortcut with Apple's keyboard.

For example, myaddr could expand into your mailing address. This works best for a small set of stable phrases. It does not save text just because you copied it, and support can differ between keyboards and text fields.

Clipboard privacy and Full Access

Copied text can include passwords, verification codes, personal messages, and financial details. Avoid turning a reusable-text collection into a store of sensitive information; use a password manager for credentials.

Full Access is a keyboard permission, not a guarantee about an app's privacy practices. Review the keyboard developer's policy before enabling it. In Typez, core typing and cursor navigation work without Full Access. Optional clipboard functionality needs it because of iOS restrictions.

Typez stores saved clipboard items locally and does not include your typing or clipboard contents in analytics. Enabling Full Access does not cause Typez to upload those contents. Removing the app can remove locally saved items, so keep another copy of anything important. Read the Typez privacy policy for details.
